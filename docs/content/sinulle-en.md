## Sing and speak with ease!

Do you suffer from performing anxiety?
Does your speaking voice tire out easily?
Would you like a more expressive voice?

The same principles underlie both the singing voice and the speaking voice. A more relaxed body produces a more confident and convincing sound. Singers may benefit from doing exercises through speaking, and speakers through singing. Together, we will explore approaches that feel natural to you and help you trust both your voice and stage presence in challenging situations.

## Do you feel that singing isn't for you?

Have you been told to stop singing because you were off-key?
Do you hesitate open your mouth because of bad experiences?

If so, you're not alone. Many people carry similar experiences for years before they decide to explore singing in a supportive and respectful environment.

Singing lessons can be a way to reconnect with your voice at your own pace and rediscover the enjoyment of singing — without pressure or unrealistic expectations.

Even experienced singers can struggle with a harsh inner critic. A strongly performance-oriented or perfectionistic mindset may also place unnecessary strain on the voice. In lessons, we focus on creating positive, practical experiences that support a fresh and constructive approach to singing.

As a teacher, I don't judge singers by their level of experience. Beginners and advanced singers are equally welcome. With appropriate guidance and training, singers can continue to develop at any age, and challenges related to vocal technique or coordination can often be addressed. Ear training is also a skill that can be developed over time.

## Sing karaoke or classical in style!

Would you like to take your singing to the next level?
Are you ready to refine your performances and deepen your expression?
Would you like to deliver your solos with ease and confidence?

Whatever your genre, we build technique and expression so that your voice carries from heart to heart. I have coached singers with a wide range of musical styles: pop, rock, rap, dance music and gospel. My students have included songwriters, church musicians, choir singers, and lieder performers.

My special expertise lies in Baroque vocal style and coloratura technique. Repertoire is always chosen based on the student's own interests and musical goals.

During lessons you'll have access to a sound system and microphone. You can use your own karaoke backing tracks through a high-quality speaker. Lessons can also include accompaniment with a professional concert pianist or an experienced pianist in popular styles.

Each year I organize several student concerts where you have the opportunity to perform alongside others. We prepare for these concerts in a focused yet unhurried, project-based way. Student concerts offer a safe and supportive environment to challenge yourself, share the joy of singing, and grow as a performer.

## Experience the wellbeing benefits of singing

Are you longing for a moment to pause and breathe?
Do you feel the need for more balance in a performance-driven everyday life?
Would you like to reconnect with yourself through your voice?

You are welcome to singing lessons even without specific musical goals. Singing has well-documented benefits for both mental and physical wellbeing. When the breath becomes freer, it supports the nervous system and brings ease to both body and mind. Many experience working with the voice and breath as calming, grounding — even meditative.

Lessons can offer a genuine breathing space in the midst of a busy life. Here, you have permission to slow down, to be present, and to explore your voice at your own pace. I meet you with calm, accepting presence and without pressure, listening carefully to what feels important in each moment.

All emotions and bodily sensations are welcome. Sharing a safe and respectful space can feel deeply restorative — even though singing lessons are not therapy.

## Riikka – Voice Teacher

I graduated as a music pedagogue from Oulu University of Applied Sciences in 2014 and completed a Master of Arts degree at the University of Lapland in 2022. I have worked as a vocal soloist, given my own concerts, and sung in high-level choirs. Teaching has always felt like the most meaningful part of my work.

My background is in classical singing, but I approach different styles with openness. Rather than shaping voices according to fixed ideals, I aim to help each singer find a way of using their voice that feels authentic and sustainable. My earlier work as a journalist developed my ability to express complex processes clearly, which supports my work as a teacher.

Questions of vocal wellbeing and expressive freedom are central to my teaching. My interest in these themes began through working with my own vocal challenges. Since then, I have drawn on both research and practical experience to support healthy, nuanced voice use. Many students describe the lessons as strengthening and supportive — sometimes even therapeutic, although they are not therapy.

Over the years, I have worked with many students from academic backgrounds, who value a space where intellectual intensity can soften into embodied presence.

I teach privately and currently work under the cooperative Lilith. I have also coached advanced choirs, including the Oulu Diocese Oratorio Choir and the vocal ensemble Sofia Magdalena, and collaborated with pianists such as Markus Vaara, Laura Heikkilä and Miikka Lehtoaho.

## Embodied and nervous-system oriented approach

The singer's instrument is the whole body–mind system. Various situational factors can interfere with vocal freedom. Some influences are more long-term in nature, such as unhelpful habits, limiting beliefs, or emotional tension.

One of the foundations of my teaching is the **Alexander Technique** — a method for recognizing and reducing unconstructive patterns of use. In Alexander terminology, this is sometimes described as "letting yourself be," meaning a willingness to release unnecessary effort, excessive goal-orientation, and the need to control.

When the body lets go of excess tension, a more balanced and sustainable vocal technique can emerge naturally. The process of freeing the voice often begins with the breath.

In my master's thesis at Oulu University of Applied Sciences, I explored the effects of the Alexander Technique on singing.

In my vocal pedagogy I draw from the **Polyvagal Theory** and trauma sensitive approach with its applications.

What matters most is creating a space in which both body and mind can feel safe and at ease — a state often referred to as the ventral vagal state within the autonomic nervous system.

In singing lessons, we work on strengthening the connection between body and mind and on developing awareness of the stress response. When the nervous system is well regulated, healthy vocal technique can emerge more naturally. Singing becomes easier, more enjoyable, and more genuinely expressive.
